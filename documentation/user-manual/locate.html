Field operation

LOCATE a tag

The LOCATE view is designed for touch operation. It scans USB-connected anchors, runs trilateration in the background, and shows the current solution on a compass-like dial.

Start a scan

  1. Tap the search icon to open the tag keypad.
  2. Enter the tag ID. DEL removes the last digit; CE clears the entry. IDs are unsigned 16-bit values (0–65535); entry can contain up to five digits.
  3. Check the ID, then tap SCAN. The app discovers connected anchors and selects configured front/right/left main anchors; a recognized spare may also be connected.
  4. The scan opens by waking the tag over LoRa: one anchor sends a wake-up packet addressed to the tag, and the station repeats it every two seconds until an anchor hears the tag on UWB or LoRa. The header shows WAKING TAG · LoRa ×n meanwhile (n = packets sent) and the dial stays empty. Once the tag answers, ranging continues as normal; if the tag goes quiet for three seconds, the wake-ups resume. Tags without the sleep firmware ignore the packet. The toggle is under ADVANCED → Configuration → Wake-up and can be flipped mid-scan.
  5. Return to the locate dial using the on-screen link. The header shows selected tag, search state and recording indicator.
  6. Tap STOP SCAN on the keypad screen to end ranging and release the anchor ports.
During a scan, anchor ports are held by the trilateration worker. The Advanced connection bar cannot open a port that is in use. Stop the scan before switching to a single-device connection.

Read the dial

The dial shows direction and distance estimates from the station anchors. It also reports signal-derived direction/confidence, recent range state, packet loss, anchor presence, tag ID and battery information when reported. Readouts marked held/stale are retained briefly after missed rounds; they are not a fresh measurement.

UWB range / bearing

Computed from anchor-to-tag ranging and configured geometry. FLY displays the UWB bearing needle; MOB is arc-only for direction, though the measured range remains available. Invalid or insufficient ranges can withhold a fix rather than draw a misleading direction.

LoRa direction arc

Uses relative anchor signal levels as a directional cue. It depends on anchor roles and calibration; an uncalibrated station warns that the arc may be unreliable.

Anchor status

Presence indicators are probed while idle and derive from live rounds during a scan. A spare is connected for redundancy/status but does not normally range each round.

Station orientation: MOB and FLY

  • MOB is the mobile station mounting. It is upright: physical left remains left. The dial uses the LoRa arc for direction in MOB mode; it does not draw the UWB bearing needle.
  • FLY is the fixed/regular station mounting, installed upside down. The app mirrors left/right station geometry for the solve.
  • These buttons affect the current session. Set mode = mob or mode = fly in hitan_station.cfg for the startup default. Missing/unknown values default to MOB.

Brightness is adjusted with the side-rail slider. It writes to hardware where permitted and otherwise dims the application in software. The rail also presents station/anchor status and additional diagnostic views.

The High/Low bar is the tag's LoRa signal level, scaled to the selected mounting: in MOB the bottom is the weakest level seen at the far end of a walk and the top is the level at the tag (about 5 dB per segment, so the bar runs cold to hot over the whole walk); in FLY the bottom is the edge of the link and the top is about 100 m out. Both windows are raw-RSSI knobs under Advanced (Signal bar): lower the MOB bottom if the bar goes dark before the tag is lost. Below it the hot/cold marker compares the level with its own value a look-back ago (one minute by default): a green up-triangle means the signal is stronger than it was then (walk on), a red down-triangle means weaker, a dash means no clear change. Beyond about 20 m from the tag the level barely changes with distance and fading swings it by a segment or more, so the marker is the signal to steer by there; expect it to answer a change of direction within about half the look-back. RESET restarts the comparison. The bar averaging time, the look-back and the dB it takes to light the marker are knobs under Advanced (Hot/cold), next to the signal-bar window.

Record and replay

In ADVANCED → Configuration, leave .hpb recording enabled (default) before starting if the run should be saved. Live scan data is written under recordings/<date>/, initially as a partial file and finalized with a round count on stop. From the Configuration tab, use Open trilat .hpb…, then Play recording to open it on the LOCATE dial. Playback controls appear beside the dial.

Trilateration files contain per-anchor round data. Single-anchor TWR recordings do not contain enough information for the locate dial; open those in Snapshots & Analysis.

Brightness, themes and navigation

The top mode strip switches between LOCATE and ADVANCED. The Advanced connection bar includes the day/night palette toggle; it is independent of the black-cat documentation theme. LOCATE automatically scales the interface to fit the window/tablet.

Good practice: verify tag ID, anchor IDs, physical orientation and station geometry before treating a direction as actionable. Watch loss/confidence indicators.
